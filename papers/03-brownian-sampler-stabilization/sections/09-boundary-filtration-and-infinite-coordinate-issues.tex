\section{Boundary, filtration, and infinite-coordinate issues}

\subsection{The final threshold contact}
Almost surely, the signed margin in \eqref{eq:signed-margin} equals zero at its
last boundary contact.  With the strict decoder convention, a target-$0$
coordinate is correct at that contact, whereas a target-$1$ coordinate is
incorrect.  Definition~\ref{def:last-error} avoids any ambiguity: every time
strictly greater than $T_i$ is correct, and the strict and non-strict
last-passage functionals have the same law by Lemma~\ref{lem:last-passage}.

\subsection{The last-error time is not a stopping time}
Let $R(t)=B(t)-t/2$ and let $T$ be its last visit to the nonnegative half-line
(or its last positive time; the conditional formula is the same).  With
$\cF_t=\sigma(R(s):0\le s\le t)$, the hitting formula
\eqref{eq:hitting-probability} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-certificate}
  \P(T\le t\mid\cF_t)
  =\1\{R(t)<0\}\bigl(1-e^{R(t)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This conditional probability takes values strictly between $0$ and $1$ with
positive probability.  Hence $\{T\le t\}$ is not $\cF_t$-measurable, and $T$
is not a stopping time.  The exact horizons in
Corollary~\ref{cor:horizons} are therefore probabilistic certification times,
not pathwise online certificates.

\subsection{No common finite time on infinitely many equal-rate coordinates}
If $I$ is countably infinite and all coordinates have unit rate, then every
$T_i$ is finite almost surely, but
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinite-supremum}
  \sup_{i\in I}T_i=\infty
  \quad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for every finite $t$, $F(t)<1$, so the probability that all infinitely
many independent stabilization marks are at most $t$ is zero.  Taking a
countable union over integer $t$ proves \eqref{eq:infinite-supremum}.

\subsection{Continuous and discrete inspection are different}
The law obtained here concerns the continuous-time readout
$t\mapsto\1\{Z_i(t)>t/2\}$.  If one inspects the sampler only at integer times,
the last observed error is bounded above by $T_i$ but has a different
lattice-valued distribution.  The asymptotic slope readout in
\cite{OpenAI2026FUSF} needs only integer times; the exact stabilization law
uses the full reconstructed continuous path.
